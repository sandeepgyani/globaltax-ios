✈️ Golden Rules for Expatriates & Multinational Employees

  • The 183-day rule: most countries make you a tax resident if you spend 183+ days there in a year — then your worldwide income usually becomes taxable there.
  • Double Tax Treaties (DTAA): if two countries tax the same income, treaties + foreign tax credits normally prevent paying twice. Keep proof of tax paid abroad.
  • US citizens are special: the USA taxes by citizenship. A US citizen in India still files a US return every year (Form 1040 + FBAR for foreign accounts > $10,000), though the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (~$130,000) and credits usually wipe out double tax.
  • Exit & arrival years: the year you move is usually a split-year — you may need to file part-year returns in both countries.
  • Indians abroad (NRIs): India taxes NRIs only on Indian-source income (rent, interest, capital gains in India). File an Indian return if that income crosses the basic exemption or TDS refunds are due.

📚 The Five Taxation Models of the World

Tax SystemHow it worksCountries using it
Progressive slab PROGRESSIVEHigher income = higher marginal tax rate (bands from 0% up to 45%+)India, USA, UK, Canada, Australia, Germany, France, Japan
Flat tax FLATSame percentage regardless of income — simple, less redistributionHungary (15%), Romania (10%), Bulgaria (10%), Estonia
Territorial TERRITORIALOnly income earned inside the country is taxed; foreign income usually tax-freeSingapore, Hong Kong, Panama, Costa Rica
Worldwide (residence-based) WORLDWIDETax residents pay on their global income, with foreign tax credit reliefIndia, UK, Australia, Canada, most of Europe
Citizenship-based CITIZENSHIPCitizens pay/file no matter where they live in the worldUSA (the famous example)

ApproachWhat happensExamples

Employer withholdingTax deducted from salary through the year; filing settles small balance/refundUSA (W-2), Canada, UK (PAYE), India (TDS)
Pre-filled returnsGovernment pre-fills your return from employer & bank data — you just approve onlineDenmark, Sweden, Finland

🏝️ Countries With No Personal Income Tax

Funded instead by oil revenue, sovereign wealth, tourism or indirect taxes: United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Bahamas, Monaco. Residents still pay VAT, municipal fees or social levies — “no income tax” never means “no tax at all”.
